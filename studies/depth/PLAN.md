# Does MateHunter's advantage grow with depth? Plan

Written 2026-09-17, before any engine ran on the positions below.

## Why

Held-out round 1 (`rounds/round-1/`) found no advantage for MateHunter 19 over
stock Stockfish 19 on 4,934 generated positions no engine could have seen, where
ChestUCI, which is in-sample for MateHunter, showed a large one. Round 1's pool
stops at mate in 9, with 44 positions at mate in 8 or more, while the ChestUCI
gain was measured from mate in 10. So one explanation, that the advantage only
appears with depth, was left open.

## Measurement, fixed now

- **Engines:** `manifests/matehunter-19.json`, `manifests/stockfish-19.json`,
  `manifests/huntsman-1.json`, unchanged.
- **Runner:** `bench/submit.py`, one thread, verification by MateProver 0.2.0 at
  1,000,000 nodes.
- **Budgets:** 10,000,000, 100,000 and 10,000 nodes, as in round 1.
- **Comparisons:** MateHunter 19 against Stockfish 19, and against Huntsman 1, run
  separately for each stated mate length from 1 to 8 (`--min-depth N --max-depth
  N`), so every depth has its own discordant pairs and sign test.
- **Combined with round 1:** round 1's positions at mate in 7 and 8 were already
  measured under the same protocol; the report gives this study's numbers alone
  and the two together.

## What would count

If the ChestUCI advantage were depth-driven and started below mate in 10,
MateHunter's discordant-pair balance against Stockfish 19 at 10,000,000 nodes
should rise with depth and favour MateHunter by mate in 8.

- A balance favouring MateHunter at mate in 8, with p < 0.05, supports depth as
  at least part of the explanation.
- No such balance at mate in 8 is evidence against depth explaining the gain in
  this range. It cannot exclude an effect that begins at mate in 10 or deeper,
  and the report will say so.
- Budgets of 100,000 and 10,000 nodes are reported the same way, and read as
  secondary.
